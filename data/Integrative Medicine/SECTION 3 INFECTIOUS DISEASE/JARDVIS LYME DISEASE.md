### **Summary of Key Information: Lyme Disease**

---

### **Epidemiology**

* **Causative Agent**: *Borrelia burgdorferi* (North America); other species (*B. garinii, B. afzelii*) prevalent in Europe and Asia.  
* **Incidence**: \~300,000 diagnoses/year in the U.S., with \~30,000 reported to the CDC annually.  
* **High-Risk Areas**: Northeastern and Midwestern U.S., 95% of cases occur in 14 states.  
* **Vectors**: *Ixodes* ticks (deer ticks); primarily in nymphal stage during late spring/summer.  
* **Transmission**: Ticks must remain attached for 24–48 hours.  
* **Populations at Risk**: Children aged 5–15 years, adults \>50 years.

---

### **Clinical Course**

1. **Early Localized Lyme Disease**:  
   * *Hallmark*: Erythema migrans (bull's-eye rash).  
   * *Symptoms*: Fever, headache, fatigue, localized lymphadenopathy.  
2. **Early Disseminated Lyme Disease** (3–5 weeks post-bite):  
   * *Manifestations*: Multiple rashes, neurological issues (e.g., cranial nerve palsies, meningitis), carditis (heart block).  
3. **Late Lyme Disease**:  
   * *Symptoms*: Arthritis (mono-/oligoarticular, large joints), polyneuropathy, encephalopathy.  
4. **Chronic/Persistent Symptoms**:  
   * Fatigue, arthralgia, myalgia; may persist in 10–20% of cases post-treatment.

---

### **Diagnosis**

* **Criteria**:  
  * *Erythema migrans* or at least one late manifestation with lab confirmation.  
  * Serological testing (two-tiered: ELISA followed by Western blot).  
* **Timing**: Sensitivity improves in later stages of Lyme disease.  
* **Testing Limitations**: False positives/negatives; seropositivity may indicate past exposure, not active infection.

---

### **Treatment**

#### **Pharmaceuticals**

* **First-Line Antibiotics**:  
  * Doxycycline: 100 mg BID for 14 days.  
  * Amoxicillin: 500 mg TID for 14 days.  
  * Cefuroxime axetil: 500 mg BID for 14 days.  
* **Prophylaxis**: Single dose of 200 mg doxycycline within 72 hours of tick removal in endemic areas.  
* **Persistent Arthritis**: 4-week course of oral antibiotics or IV therapy for non-responders.

#### **Supplements**

* **Probiotics**: Prevent antibiotic-associated diarrhea (e.g., *Lactobacillus rhamnosus*, *S. boulardii*).  
* **Omega-3 Fatty Acids**: Reduce inflammation (2500 mg/day).  
* **Intravenous Micronutrients**: Myers’ cocktail for symptom management.

#### **Lifestyle & Integrative Therapies**

* **Exercise**: Moderate aerobic and weight training 2–3x/week.  
* **Mindfulness-Based Stress Reduction**: 8-week program.  
* **Massage & Acupuncture**: Symptomatic relief for pain and fatigue.  
* **Diet**: Anti-inflammatory (e.g., omega-3-rich foods, whole grains).

---

### **Prevention Prescription**

* **Tick Avoidance**:  
  * Wear protective clothing; apply DEET/permethrin.  
  * Check for ticks and remove promptly.  
  * Clear vegetation in outdoor areas.  
* **Prophylaxis**:  
  * Antibiotic dose post-bite if tick attachment \>48 hours.

---

### **Chronic Lyme Disease (Controversial)**

* **Symptoms**: Fatigue, pain, depression, neurological complaints.  
* **Challenges**: No consistent diagnostic criteria or evidence of active infection.  
* **Approach**: Symptomatic management; avoid prolonged antibiotic use without confirmation of active infection.

### **Pathophysiology**

Constipation is defined by a variety of factors, including difficulty with bowel movements, infrequent stools, or hard stools. It is categorized into three main types:

1. **Normal-Transit Constipation**:  
   * The stool passes through the colon at a normal rate, but patients perceive difficulty with evacuation or experience hard stools.  
2. **Slow-Transit Constipation**:  
   * Prolonged stool transit time through the colon, often accompanied by abdominal bloating and infrequent bowel movements.  
3. **Pelvic Floor Dysfunction**:  
   * Involves uncoordinated stool evacuation, often with a sensation of incomplete evacuation or anorectal blockage.

Secondary causes such as hypothyroidism, medications (e.g., opioids, anticholinergics, calcium channel blockers), or lifestyle factors (e.g., inadequate physical activity, poor diet) may exacerbate constipation.

---

### **Diagnostic Criteria**

**Rome II Criteria for Functional Constipation**:

* Straining, hard stools, incomplete evacuation, anorectal blockage, or use of manual maneuvers in ≥25% of defecations.  
* Fewer than three bowel movements per week.

### **Integrative Management Strategies**

#### **1\. Lifestyle and Behavior**

* **Physical Activity**:  
  * Regular exercise reduces constipation risk and improves symptoms. Encourage at least 30 minutes of moderate activity daily.  
* **Behavioral Training**:  
  * Use a footstool to achieve a squatting posture during bowel movements, which optimizes rectal angle and facilitates defecation.

---

#### **2\. Nutrition**

* **Fiber**:  
  * Recommended daily intake: 20–25 g for adults; for children, use the formula: *Age \+ 5 grams*.  
  * Sources: Whole grains, fruits, vegetables, legumes, flaxseed, bran.  
  * Gradual increase in fiber intake is essential to minimize bloating.  
* **Fluid Intake**:  
  * Adequate hydration is critical, particularly when increasing fiber intake. Aim for 8–10 glasses of water daily.  
* **Elimination Diets**:  
  * Trial elimination of dairy (4–6 weeks) for suspected intolerances.  
* **Juices with Sorbitol**:  
  * Prune, pear, or apple juice can increase stool frequency due to their osmotic effects.

---

#### **3\. Supplements**

* **Fiber Supplements**:  
  * Options include psyllium (5–10 g/day), methylcellulose, or polycarbophil.  
  * Psyllium is particularly effective for idiopathic constipation.  
* **Probiotics**:  
  * Strains such as *Lactobacillus casei Shirota* and *Bifidobacterium* (≥10⁸ CFU/day) improve stool frequency and consistency.  
* **Magnesium Citrate**:  
  * Start with two 150 mg capsules at bedtime, adjusting as needed to achieve soft stools.

---

#### **4\. Pharmaceuticals**

* **First-Line Osmotic Laxatives**:  
  * **Polyethylene Glycol (PEG)**:  
    * Adults: 17–34 g daily; children: 8.5 g (4 oz water) once daily.  
  * **Lactulose**: 15–30 mL daily.  
* **Stimulant Laxatives**:  
  * Options include senna (8.6–30 mg daily) or bisacodyl (10–15 mg orally or rectal suppository).  
  * Use cautiously due to potential for dependency.  
* **Stool Softeners**:  
  * Docusate sodium (50–400 mg/day) facilitates water absorption into stools.  
* **Newer Agents**:  
  * **Lubiprostone** (24 mcg twice daily) and **Linaclotide** (145 mcg daily) stimulate intestinal fluid secretion for chronic constipation or IBS-C.

---

#### **5\. Mind-Body and Adjunctive Therapies**

* **Biofeedback**:  
  * Effective for pelvic floor dysfunction, improving muscle coordination and evacuation.  
* **Hypnotherapy**:  
  * Promotes relaxation and mitigates stress-induced constipation.  
* **Abdominal Massage**:  
  * Increases bowel motility and alleviates symptoms with consistent use.  
* **Acupuncture**:  
  * Shown to improve bowel frequency in chronic constipation.

---

#### **6\. Botanicals**

* **Aloe Vera**:  
  * Anthraquinones in aloe latex act as natural laxatives. Dose: 50 mg extract at bedtime.  
* **Traditional Chinese Medicine**:  
  * Hemp seed pills and other herbal formulations show promise but require further research.

---

#### **7\. Pediatric-Specific Considerations**

* Encourage daily toilet time after meals and avoid punitive approaches to accidents.  
* Fiber intake: Use the age \+ 5 formula for daily grams.  
* Probiotics (e.g., *Lactobacillus reuteri*) and polyethylene glycol are first-line treatments.

---

### **Emerging Therapies**

* **Fecal Microbiota Transplantation (FMT)**:  
  * An experimental therapy for severe, refractory constipation, offering potential benefits through microbiota restoration.

---

### **Prevention**

* Eat high-fiber foods and minimize processed, high-fat, and low-fiber options.  
* Maintain adequate hydration.  
* Engage in regular physical activity.  
* Use the gastrocolic reflex after meals for bowel movements.

### **Pathophysiology of Carpal Tunnel Syndrome (CTS)**

Carpal Tunnel Syndrome (CTS) is a **compressive neuropathy of the median nerve**, which is affected by anatomical, physiological, and occupational factors. It predominantly occurs in women (3:1 ratio) after the age of 30\.

---

### **Mechanism of CTS**

1. **Anatomy**:  
   * The carpal tunnel is formed by three carpal bones and the flexor retinaculum, containing nine flexor tendons and the median nerve.  
   * Any reduction in the tunnel's space (e.g., edema, tendon inflammation, or bony overgrowth) increases pressure, compressing the median nerve and blood vessels.  
2. **Pathophysiology**:  
   * **Acute compression** causes transient ischemia of the nerve, leading to pain, tingling, and numbness.  
   * **Chronic compression** results in prolonged ischemia, decreased nerve conduction, and atrophy of the thenar eminence.  
3. **Contributing Factors**:  
   * **Functional**: Repetitive stress activities, such as typing or use of vibratory tools.  
   * **Structural**: Trauma, subluxation of the lunate bone, or systemic conditions like hypothyroidism, obesity, or diabetes.

---

### **Clinical Manifestations**

1. **Symptoms**:  
   * Pain, numbness, and tingling in the median nerve distribution (thumb, index, middle, and half of the ring finger).  
   * Night pain and relief by "shaking" the hands.  
   * Weakness in thumb opposition and thenar eminence wasting in advanced cases.  
2. **Examination Findings**:  
   * Positive **Phalen test** (numbness induced by wrist flexion) or **Tinel’s sign** (tingling upon percussion of the wrist).  
   * Decreased sensation to pinprick in the median nerve distribution.  
3. **Differential Diagnosis**:  
   * Other compressive neuropathies (e.g., cervical radiculopathy, thoracic outlet syndrome) and systemic causes.

---

### **Diagnostic Criteria**

According to the National Institute for Occupational Safety and Health (NIOSH), CTS is defined by at least two of the following:

1. Median nerve distribution symptoms (paresthesia, pain, numbness).  
2. Physical findings of median nerve compression (Phalen or Tinel test).  
3. Electrodiagnostic evidence of median nerve dysfunction across the carpal tunnel.

---

### **Management of CTS**

#### **1\. Lifestyle and Behavioral Modifications**

* **Activity Changes**:  
  * Avoid repetitive wrist movements and sustained gripping.  
  * Take breaks during repetitive tasks.  
* **Splinting**:  
  * Nighttime splints maintaining a neutral wrist position are first-line options.  
  * Properly fitted, rigid splints are preferred.  
* **Exercise**:  
  * Stretching and strengthening exercises for the hands and wrists may help alleviate symptoms.

---

#### **2\. Non-Surgical Interventions**

* **Pharmaceuticals**:  
  * **NSAIDs**: Offer pain relief, although their anti-inflammatory benefits for CTS are inconclusive.  
  * **Oral Steroids**: Prednisolone 20 mg/day for 2 weeks has shown symptom relief.  
  * **Steroid Injections**: Methylprednisolone acetate injected into the carpal canal provides short-term relief.  
* **Supplements and Botanicals**:  
  * **Vitamin B6**: May help in cases of mild deficiency-related neuropathy.  
  * **Ginger**: Anti-inflammatory properties may alleviate pain, especially in concurrent conditions like arthritis.  
* **Ultrasound Therapy**:  
  * Effective for mild to moderate cases, reducing inflammation and improving symptoms.  
* **Osteopathic Manipulative Treatment (OMT)**:  
  * Techniques targeting the transverse carpal ligament can relieve pressure on the median nerve.  
* **Acupuncture**:  
  * Traditional acupuncture is considered a viable adjunctive therapy for mild CTS.

---

#### **3\. Surgical Intervention**

Surgical decompression is indicated for:

* Severe, unrelenting pain or numbness.  
* Thenar eminence atrophy or loss of motor function.  
* Failure of non-surgical treatments after 6–12 months.

**Surgical Options**:

* **Open Surgery**: Involves a larger incision but allows for clear visualization.  
* **Mini-Open Surgery**: Uses a smaller incision.  
* **Endoscopic Surgery**: Minimally invasive with faster recovery.

**Considerations**:

* Pregnant patients should avoid surgery as symptoms often resolve postpartum.

---

### **Therapeutic Overview**

1. **Mild to Moderate CTS**:  
   * Start with behavioral modifications, splinting, and non-surgical therapies.  
   * Reassess after 3 weeks; if symptoms persist, consider corticosteroid injections or referral for advanced therapy.  
2. **Advanced CTS with Thenar Atrophy**:  
   * Prompt referral for surgical evaluation.  
   * Ensure accurate diagnosis with electrodiagnostic studies.  
3. **Prevention Strategies**:  
   * Ergonomic workplace adjustments.  
   * Avoid prolonged wrist extension or flexion.  
   * Implement regular breaks and stretching.

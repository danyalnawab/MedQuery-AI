### **Clinical Signs and Management of Parkinson’s Disease (PD)**

---

### **Clinical Signs and Symptoms**

#### **Motor Symptoms**

1. **Cardinal Features**:  
   * Resting tremor (often unilateral, worsens with stress).  
   * Rigidity (increased muscle tone causing stiffness).  
   * Bradykinesia (slowness of movement) and akinesia (loss of movement).  
   * Postural instability (difficulty maintaining balance).  
2. **Early Signs**:  
   * Subtle motor changes: decreased arm swing, reduced stride length, and smaller handwriting.  
   * Difficulty with fine motor tasks (e.g., buttoning shirts).  
3. **Advanced Symptoms**:  
   * Shuffling gait, freezing episodes, and stooped posture.  
   * Difficulty rising from chairs or initiating movement.

#### **Non-Motor Symptoms**

1. **Psychiatric**:  
   * Depression, anxiety, apathy, dementia, and hallucinations.  
2. **Autonomic**:  
   * Constipation, orthostatic hypotension, urinary incontinence, and excessive sweating.  
3. **Sleep-related**:  
   * Insomnia, REM behavior disorder, restless legs syndrome, and fatigue.  
4. **Sensory**:  
   * Impaired sense of smell/taste, pain, blurred vision, and numbness.

---

### **Pathophysiology**

1. **Neuronal Degeneration**:  
   * Loss of dopaminergic neurons in the substantia nigra.  
   * Formation of Lewy bodies (aggregates of alpha-synuclein and ubiquitin).  
2. **The Braak Hypothesis**:  
   * Early pathology in the olfactory bulb and dorsal motor nucleus, progressing rostrally to the substantia nigra.  
3. **Mitochondrial Dysfunction**:  
   * Impaired mitochondrial function leads to oxidative stress and neuronal death.

---

### **Integrative Therapy and Management**

#### **Lifestyle and Exercise**

1. **Aerobic Exercise**:  
   * Improves physical functioning, quality of life, and may slow disease progression.  
   * Activities: walking, dancing, yoga, or tai chi.  
2. **Safety Modifications**:  
   * Use of grab bars, modified footwear, and laser-assisted walking aids.

#### **Nutrition**

1. **Dietary Recommendations**:  
   * High fiber intake to alleviate constipation.  
   * Omega-3 fatty acids (e.g., flaxseed, salmon) for neuroprotection.  
   * Antioxidant-rich foods (e.g., colorful fruits, vegetables, and nuts).  
2. **Green Tea**:  
   * 3 cups/day; may reduce the risk of PD and provide neuroprotective effects.

#### **Supplements**

1. **N-Acetyl Cysteine (NAC)**:  
   * Precursor to glutathione, 600–1200 mg/day.  
2. **Coenzyme Q10**:  
   * Neuroprotective effects; dosage of 1200 mg/day.  
3. **Vitamin D**:  
   * Maintain serum levels \>20 ng/mL.  
4. **Curcumin**:  
   * Anti-inflammatory effects; dosage: 400–600 mg 3x/day.

#### **Mind-Body Therapies**

* **Yoga and Tai Chi**:  
  * Improve balance, reduce stress, and enhance well-being.  
* **Mindfulness Meditation**:  
  * Reduces stress, depression, and anxiety.

---

### **Pharmacological Treatment**

#### **Early Symptoms**

1. **Monoamine Oxidase Inhibitors (MAO-B)**:  
   * **Rasagiline**: 1 mg/day for mild symptoms.  
2. **Amantadine**:  
   * 100 mg twice or thrice daily.

#### **Motor Symptoms**

1. **Levodopa/Carbidopa**:  
   * Gold standard for motor symptoms; start with 25/100 mg 3x/day.  
   * For advanced disease: extended-release formulations (e.g., Rytary™ or Duopa™ pump).  
2. **Dopamine Agonists**:  
   * **Ropinirole**: Start 0.25 mg TID, max 24 mg/day.  
   * **Pramipexole**: Start 0.125 mg TID, max 4.5 mg/day.  
   * **Rotigotine Patch**: Start 2 mg/24 hr, max 8 mg/24 hr.

#### **Adjunctive Therapies**

1. **COMT Inhibitors**:  
   * **Entacapone**: 200 mg with each dose of levodopa.  
2. **Anticholinergics**:  
   * **Trihexyphenidyl**: Effective for tremor refractory to other medications.

---

### **Surgical Interventions**

1. **Deep Brain Stimulation (DBS)**:  
   * Target: Subthalamic nucleus or globus pallidus internus.  
   * Indicated for patients with levodopa-responsive but poorly controlled symptoms.

---

### **Prevention and Risk Reduction**

* Regular aerobic exercise.  
* Antioxidant and omega-3 rich diet.  
* Reduce exposure to pesticides, heavy metals, and industrial solvents.  
* Maintain social and mental engagement to combat depression and apathy.

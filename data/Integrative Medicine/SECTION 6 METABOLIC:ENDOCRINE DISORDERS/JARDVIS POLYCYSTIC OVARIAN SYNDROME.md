#### **1\. Overview**

* PCOS is the most common endocrine disorder in women of reproductive age, affecting approximately 10% of women. It is often underdiagnosed.  
* Key features include oligoovulation, infertility, acne, hirsutism, and metabolic risks such as diabetes and cardiovascular disease.  
* PCOS manifests differently depending on environmental, genetic, and lifestyle factors.

#### **2\. Pathophysiology**

* **Hypothalamic-Pituitary Dysfunction:** Abnormal gonadotropin-releasing hormone (GnRH) and luteinizing hormone (LH) secretion disrupt ovarian hormone production.  
* **Ovarian Defects:** Primary defects in steroidogenesis result in hyperandrogenism.  
* **Metabolic Disorder:** Peripheral insulin resistance affects the hypothalamus, pituitary, ovaries, and adrenal glands.  
* Symptoms often develop in adolescence and worsen over time, though they may be masked by contraceptive hormones or affected by weight changes.

#### **3\. Diagnosis**

* **Criteria:** Diagnostic criteria vary but consistently include ovarian dysfunction (oligo/anovulation), hyperandrogenism (clinical or biochemical), and exclusion of related disorders.  
  * Commonly used criteria include:  
    * National Institutes of Health (1990): Oligo-ovulation and hyperandrogenism.  
    * Rotterdam (2003): Any two of three (oligo-anovulation, polycystic ovaries, hyperandrogenism).  
    * Androgen Excess Society (2006): Hyperandrogenism plus ovarian markers.  
* **Evaluation:** History, physical examination, and laboratory tests (androgen levels, antimüllerian hormone, glucose tolerance test) are essential. Pelvic ultrasound supports the diagnosis.  
* **Markers:** Elevated testosterone-to-dihydrotestosterone (TT/DHT) ratio and metabolic syndrome markers such as C-reactive protein and fibrinogen.

#### **4\. Clinical Manifestations**

* **Reproductive:** Irregular periods, infertility, and polycystic ovaries.  
* **Androgen Excess:** Acne, hirsutism, and alopecia.  
* **Metabolic:** Obesity, insulin resistance, and increased cardiovascular risk.  
* **Psychological:** Depression, anxiety, and body image issues.

#### **5\. Integrative Therapy**

* **Lifestyle Interventions:**  
  * Weight loss (5%-10%) significantly improves clinical markers.  
  * Regular exercise improves insulin sensitivity, ovulation, and reduces waist circumference.  
  * Diet: Focus on low glycemic index, high fiber, and balanced macronutrients.  
* **Nutrition:**  
  * **Omega-3 Fatty Acids:** Anti-inflammatory properties support cardiovascular health.  
  * **Soy:** Limited evidence for cholesterol improvement; moderate consumption recommended.  
  * **Supplements:**  
    * **Inositol:** Improves ovulation, insulin sensitivity, and testosterone levels.  
    * **Chromium:** Enhances insulin action and improves glucose metabolism.  
    * **Vitamin D:** Improves menstrual cycles, ovulation, and insulin resistance.  
    * **N-Acetylcysteine (NAC):** Improves ovulation rates and antioxidant status.  
    * **Selenium:** Reduces inflammation and improves insulin resistance.  
* **Botanicals:**  
  * **Cinnamon:** Reduces insulin resistance.  
  * **Licorice:** Antiandrogen effects; often combined with spironolactone.  
  * **Chaste Tree Berry (Vitex):** Balances estrogen and progesterone levels; supports ovulation.  
* **Complementary Therapies:**  
  * **Acupuncture:** Improves ovulation and reduces sympathetic nervous system tone.  
  * **Mind-Body Therapy:** Supports mental health, reduces stress, and enhances quality of life.

#### **6\. Pharmaceuticals**

* **Insulin Sensitizers:**  
  * **Metformin:** Improves insulin resistance, ovulation, and hyperandrogenism.  
  * **Thiazolidinediones:** Effective but associated with weight gain.  
* **Weight Loss Medications:**  
  * **Orlistat:** Reduces insulin resistance and testosterone levels.  
* **Hormonal Therapy:**  
  * **Oral Contraceptives:** First-line for hyperandrogenism and menstrual irregularity.  
  * **Progestins:** Protect against endometrial hyperplasia in women not using OCPs.  
  * **Antiandrogens:** Spironolactone or flutamide for hirsutism; requires contraception.  
* **Fertility Treatments:**  
  * **Clomiphene Citrate:** Effective for ovulation induction; approximately 80% of women ovulate.  
* **Statins:** Used for hyperlipidemia; additional benefits for inflammation and androgen levels.

#### **7\. Surgical Interventions**

* **Bariatric Surgery:** For morbidly obese patients with significant comorbidities.  
* **Ovarian Surgery:** Indicated for clomiphene-resistant anovulatory women.

#### **8\. Prevention and Recommendations**

* Maintain healthy weight through diet and exercise.  
* Avoid endocrine-disrupting chemicals.  
* Promote omega-3 fatty acids, low glycemic index foods, and moderate soy intake.  
* Regular screening for cardiovascular and metabolic risks.  
* Patient education on lifestyle modifications and medication adherence.

#### **9\. Therapeutic Review**

* Lifestyle modifications are foundational.  
* Supplements and botanicals offer supportive benefits.  
* Pharmacological and surgical options address severe symptoms and comorbidities.  
* Holistic and individualized care plans improve outcomes for women with PCOS.

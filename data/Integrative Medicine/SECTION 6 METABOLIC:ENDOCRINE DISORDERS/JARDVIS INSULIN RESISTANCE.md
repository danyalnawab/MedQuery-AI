### **Definition and Diagnostic Criteria**

* **Metabolic Syndrome**: A cluster of risk factors for atherosclerosis and type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM), including:  
  * Elevated fasting glucose (≥100 mg/dL)  
  * Elevated triglycerides (≥150 mg/dL)  
  * Reduced HDL cholesterol (\<40 mg/dL for men, \<50 mg/dL for women)  
  * Hypertension (≥130/85 mmHg)  
  * Central obesity (waist circumference \>40 inches for men, \>35 inches for women in Western populations)  
* Diagnosis requires **three or more** of these factors (Table 32.1).

---

### **Pathophysiology**

* **Insulin Resistance (IR)**: The primary mechanism underlying metabolic syndrome.  
  * **Skeletal Muscle IR**: Decreased glucose disposal.  
  * **Hepatic IR**: Increased glucose production via gluconeogenesis and glycogenolysis.  
  * **Pancreatic Beta-Cell Dysfunction**: Impaired insulin secretion and timing.  
  * Chronic low-grade **inflammation** and **lipotoxicity** (e.g., fatty acid accumulation in liver and muscle) perpetuate IR.  
* **Environmental Factors**: Pollutants such as BPA, persistent organic pollutants (POPs), and arsenic contribute to IR and metabolic syndrome.  
* **Nonalcoholic Fatty Liver Disease (NAFLD)**: A hepatic manifestation of metabolic syndrome linked to visceral fat and IR.

---

### **Prevalence**

* Affects **35% of U.S. adults** and up to **50% of those aged 60 years or older**.  
* Higher prevalence among:  
  * Women (35.6%) vs. Men (30.3%)  
  * Ethnicities: Hispanics (35.4%) \> Non-Hispanic Whites (33.4%) \> Blacks (32.7%).

### **Integrative Management**

#### **Lifestyle Interventions**

1. **Exercise**:  
   * Moderate aerobic activity (30–60 minutes/day, 5 days/week) and resistance training.  
   * Benefits include enhanced **GLUT4 transporter activity**, reduced inflammation, and improved glucose metabolism.  
2. **Weight Management**:  
   * Target 6–10% weight loss for significant IR improvement.  
   * Emphasize slow, mindful eating and avoidance of sedentary behaviors.  
3. **Stress Management**:  
   * Techniques such as yoga, meditation, and relaxation to lower cortisol and improve adrenal function.

#### **Nutrition**

* **Mediterranean Diet**: Emphasis on vegetables, legumes, fish, and healthy fats (e.g., olive oil, nuts).  
* **Low Glycemic Index Foods**: Whole grains, non-starchy vegetables, and fruits to preserve beta-cell function.  
* **High Fiber**: Soluble fibers (e.g., psyllium, oats, barley) to enhance glycemic control.  
* **Therapeutic Foods**:  
  * **Blueberries**: Improve IR and reduce oxidative stress.  
  * **Apple Cider Vinegar**: Lowers postprandial glucose levels.  
* **Avoid** refined starches, fast foods, sugar-sweetened beverages, and artificial sweeteners.

#### **Nutritional Supplements**

* **Core Recommendations**:  
  * Omega-3 fatty acids (1–4 g/day)  
  * Vitamin D (300–2000 IU/day)  
  * Alpha-lipoic acid (100–300 mg/day)  
  * Coenzyme Q10 (60–120 mg/day)  
  * Chromium picolinate (200–1000 mcg/day)  
* **Condition-Specific Additions**:  
  * **Vitamin C** (1000–2000 mg/day) for oxidative stress.  
  * **Magnesium** (100–382 mg/day) for insulin sensitivity.  
  * **Milk Thistle** (420–600 mg/day) for hepatic protection (NAFLD).

#### **Pharmaceuticals**

* **Insulin Sensitizers**:  
  * **Metformin**: Reduces hepatic glucose production (500–2500 mg/day).  
  * **Pioglitazone**: Enhances insulin sensitivity (15–45 mg/day).  
* **Other Options**:  
  * Alpha-glucosidase inhibitors (slow carbohydrate absorption).  
  * Sodium-glucose cotransporter-2 (SGLT-2) inhibitors (promote glucosuria).

---

### **Prevention Prescription**

* Maintain healthy weight and manage stress.  
* Follow a **Mediterranean-type diet** with low glycemic load.  
* Regularly monitor glucose, lipids, and inflammatory markers.  
* Educate patients on the significance of lifestyle and nutritional changes over pharmacological interventions.

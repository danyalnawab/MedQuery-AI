### **Acne Vulgaris: Pathophysiology, Clinical Features, and Management Overview**

---

### **Pathophysiology**

Acne vulgaris is a disorder of the pilosebaceous unit involving four primary mechanisms:

1. **Abnormal Keratinization**:  
   * Increased keratinocyte proliferation and reduced desquamation lead to follicular plugging, forming microcomedones (precursors to comedones).  
   * Can progress to closed or open comedones and eventual follicular rupture, triggering inflammation.  
2. **Increased Sebum Production**:  
   * Androgen stimulation increases sebaceous gland activity, creating a lipid-rich environment conducive to bacterial growth.  
   * Androgens are produced in adrenal glands, gonads, and sebaceous glands.  
3. **Colonization by *Propionibacterium acnes* (now *Cutibacterium acnes*)**:  
   * This anaerobic bacterium thrives in the lipid-rich environment, producing proinflammatory mediators that activate immune responses.  
4. **Inflammation**:  
   * Triggered by bacterial antigens, Toll-like receptors, cytokines, and interleukins, leading to the formation of inflammatory lesions (papules, pustules, nodules).

---

### **Management Strategies**

#### **General Measures**

* Use gentle, pH-balanced cleansers (avoid harsh scrubbing).  
* Emphasize consistent use of prescribed treatments for at least 2–3 months to evaluate effectiveness.

---

#### **Topical Therapies**

1. **Benzoyl Peroxide**:  
   * Antimicrobial and anti-inflammatory.  
   * Recommended for inflammatory acne; apply once or twice daily (2.5–5% formulations).  
   * Side effects: dryness, irritation, and bleaching of fabrics.  
2. **Retinoids**:  
   * Tretinoin, adapalene, or tazarotene reduce keratinocyte proliferation, sebum production, and comedones.  
   * Apply a pea-sized amount at bedtime.  
   * Adapalene causes less irritation and photosensitivity.  
   * Avoid during pregnancy; use sunscreen.  
3. **Topical Antibiotics**:  
   * Clindamycin or erythromycin for inflammatory acne.  
   * Use in combination with benzoyl peroxide to reduce resistance.  
   * Dapsone 5% gel: effective for inflammatory lesions; apply twice daily.  
4. **Azelaic Acid**:  
   * Antimicrobial, anti-inflammatory, and comedolytic.  
   * Apply 15–20% formulations once or twice daily.  
   * Safe for postinflammatory hyperpigmentation.  
5. **Salicylic Acid**:  
   * Mild exfoliant for comedonal acne.  
   * Apply 0.5–2% formulations once daily.

---

#### **Systemic Therapies**

1. **Oral Antibiotics**:  
   * For moderate to severe inflammatory acne.  
   * Options: doxycycline, minocycline, tetracycline, azithromycin.  
   * Combine with topical benzoyl peroxide or retinoids to prevent resistance.  
   * Limit use to 6 months if possible.  
2. **Hormonal Treatments**:  
   * Combined oral contraceptives (COCs) reduce androgen levels.  
   * Spironolactone (50–200 mg/day) blocks androgen receptors.  
   * Contraindicated in pregnancy and some cardiovascular conditions.  
3. **Oral Isotretinoin**:  
   * For severe, refractory acne.  
   * Dosage: 0.5–1 mg/kg/day for 20 weeks.  
   * Requires iPLEDGE enrollment for women of childbearing age.  
   * Side effects: teratogenicity, mucocutaneous dryness, hypertriglyceridemia.

---

#### **Integrative and Dietary Therapies**

1. **Dietary Modifications**:  
   * Low-glycemic index diet reduces insulin and IGF-1, decreasing sebum production.  
   * Avoid dairy products, especially skim milk.  
2. **Omega-3 Fatty Acids**:  
   * Anti-inflammatory properties.  
   * Dosage: 1–3 g/day (EPA+DHA).  
3. **Zinc Supplementation**:  
   * Dosage: Zinc gluconate 30 mg/day (20 mg/day for children 9–13 years).  
   * Side effects: nausea, gastrointestinal upset.  
4. **Brewer’s Yeast**:  
   * Dosage: 6 g/day (divided doses).  
   * Limited side effects, mainly gastrointestinal.  
5. **Botanicals**:  
   * **Tea Tree Oil**: Apply 5–15% formulations once or twice daily.  
   * **Green Tea**: 2–3% lotion twice daily; also consume 2–3 cups/day.  
   * **Turmeric**: Topical application (mixed with coconut oil) or oral supplementation (500 mg twice daily).

---

### **Prevention and Maintenance**

* Wash face twice daily with gentle cleansers.  
* Avoid picking or squeezing lesions to prevent scarring.  
* Use noncomedogenic skincare and cosmetics.  
* Manage stress through relaxation techniques.

### **Conclusion**

Acne vulgaris is a multifactorial condition requiring individualized treatment. A combination of topical and systemic therapies, lifestyle modifications, and dietary adjustments can significantly improve outcomes and prevent recurrence.

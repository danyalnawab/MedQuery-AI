### **Recurrent Aphthous Ulcers (RAUs): Overview of Pathophysiology, Clinical Features, and Management**

#### **Pathophysiology**

* **Defining Characteristics**:  
  * Recurrent, painful oral ulcers with erythematous halos and gray/yellow floors.  
  * Typically occur on nonkeratinized or poorly keratinized mucosa.  
* **Key Mechanism**:  
  * Immune-mediated inflammation.  
  * Involves IgE-bearing lymphocytes, mast cells, and elevated TNF-alpha.  
  * Cytotoxic lymphocytes and monocytes drive ulceration.  
* **Clinical Variations**:  
  * **Minor RAUs**: \<5 mm, heal without scarring in 7–14 days.  
  * **Major RAUs**: \>5 mm, deeper, painful, heal over 6 weeks with scarring.  
  * **Herpetiform RAUs**: 1–3 mm clusters (10–100), heal in 7–14 days without scarring.

---

#### **Clinical Features**

* **Symptoms**:  
  * Painful ulcers with a red halo.  
  * Difficulty eating, drinking, or speaking depending on ulcer severity.  
* **Etiology**:  
  * Multifactorial causes:  
    * **Genetic predisposition**: Familial tendency.  
    * **Nutritional deficiencies**: B1, B2, B6, B12, iron, folic acid.  
    * **Systemic diseases**: Celiac, Crohn’s, HIV, Behçet’s syndrome.  
    * **Environmental factors**: Stress, food allergies, toothpaste additives (e.g., sodium lauryl sulfate).  
    * **Medications**: NSAIDs, ACE inhibitors, metformin.  
* **Diagnosis**:  
  * Based on history and clinical presentation.  
  * Rule out systemic conditions like Behçet’s syndrome or nutritional deficiencies.

---

#### **Management Strategies**

##### **Lifestyle Adjustments**

* Avoid sodium lauryl sulfate-containing toothpaste.  
* Maintain oral hygiene while avoiding trauma from brushing or rough foods.

##### **Nutritional Interventions**

* Correct deficiencies:  
  * Vitamin B complex (B1, B2, B6, B12).  
  * Iron and folic acid supplementation.  
* Gluten-free diet for those with celiac disease or gluten sensitivity.  
* Avoid triggers like acidic foods, spicy meals, and common allergens.

---

#### **Pharmaceutical Management**

* **Topical Treatments**:  
  * **Amlexanox (Aphthasol)**: Anti-inflammatory paste applied 4x daily.  
  * **Triamcinolone (Kenalog in Orabase)**: Reduces inflammation and promotes healing.  
  * **Viscous Lidocaine**: Provides pain relief; swish before meals.  
  * **Chlorhexidine Gluconate**: Reduces ulcer duration; rinse twice daily.  
  * **Tetracycline-Fluocinolone-Diphenhydramine Mouthwash**: Combines antimicrobial, anti-inflammatory, and anesthetic effects for severe ulcers.  
* **Systemic Treatments**:  
  * **Colchicine**: Effective for severe cases or Behçet’s syndrome.  
  * **Prednisone**: Short-term use for severe exacerbations.  
  * **Thalidomide**: Reserved for intractable cases due to significant side effects.  
  * **Tumor Necrosis Factor (TNF) Inhibitors**: For resistant autoimmune-related cases.  
* **Cautery**:  
  * Silver nitrate sticks for pain reduction, although they do not expedite healing.

---

#### **Integrative and Alternative Therapies**

* **Nutritional Supplements**:  
  * Vitamin B12 (1000 mcg daily): Effective even in normal B12 levels.  
  * Glutamine: Swish and swallow for mucosal support during chemotherapy or severe cases.  
* **Botanicals**:  
  * **Licorice (Glycyrrhiza)**: Mouthwash or dissolvable disks (e.g., CankerMelt).  
  * **Chamomile**: Anti-inflammatory rinse (10–15 drops in warm water).  
  * **Honey**: Proven to reduce ulcer severity, especially in cancer-related mucositis.  
* **Mind-Body Techniques**:  
  * Stress management through meditation, guided imagery, or hypnosis.

---

#### **Prevention and Long-Term Care**

* Avoid trauma, irritants, and trigger foods.  
* Address underlying nutritional deficiencies.  
* Consider B vitamin supplementation for prevention.  
* Promote stress reduction to mitigate recurrence.

---

### **Conclusion**

RAUs are a multifactorial condition requiring a tailored approach to management. Early diagnosis, identification of underlying causes, and a combination of topical, systemic, and integrative therapies can effectively manage symptoms and prevent recurrence. Chronic and severe cases may benefit from systemic immunosuppressants or biologic therapies under careful medical supervision.

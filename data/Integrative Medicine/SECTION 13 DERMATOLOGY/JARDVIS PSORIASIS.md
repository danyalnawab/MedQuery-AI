### **Psoriasis: Pathophysiology, Clinical Presentation, and Management Overview**

---

### **Pathophysiology**

1. **Epidermal Hyperproliferation**:  
   * Abnormal differentiation and overproduction of epidermal keratinocytes.  
   * Characterized by **thick plaques with silvery scales**.  
2. **Immune Dysregulation**:  
   * T-cell-mediated disorder involving **type 1 helper T cells (Th1)** and **type 17 helper T cells (Th17)**.  
   * Key cytokines include **IL-2**, **TNF-alpha**, **IL-17**, **IL-23**, and **IFN-gamma**, which promote inflammation and keratinocyte proliferation.  
   * **Antigen-presenting cells (APCs)** activate T cells, sometimes through superantigens (e.g., streptococcal triggers in guttate psoriasis).  
3. **Genetic Factors**:  
   * Risk increases significantly if one (14%) or both (41%) parents have psoriasis.  
   * Associated with specific **HLA haplotypes** and genetic loci.  
4. **Environmental Triggers**:  
   * **Physical trauma** (Koebner phenomenon).  
   * **Infections**: Group A streptococcal pharyngitis.  
   * **Lifestyle factors**: Smoking, alcohol use, obesity.  
   * **Medications**: Lithium, beta-blockers, antimalarials.  
   * **Stress** exacerbates immune activation and disease severity.  
5. **Systemic Associations**:  
   * Increased risk of **metabolic syndrome**, **coronary artery disease**, and **psoriatic arthritis**.

---

### **Clinical Presentation**

1. **Common Symptoms**:  
   * Erythematous plaques with silvery scales.  
   * Commonly affects the scalp, elbows, knees, umbilicus, and gluteal cleft.  
   * **Pruritus**: Variable in intensity.  
2. **Subtypes of Psoriasis**:  
   * **Chronic Plaque Psoriasis**: Most common (90%), involving stable plaques.  
   * **Guttate Psoriasis**: Drop-like lesions triggered by streptococcal infection, common in children and young adults.  
   * **Inverse Psoriasis**: Affects flexural areas with less scaling, prone to secondary infections.  
   * **Pustular Psoriasis**: Confluent pustules on erythematous skin; can be life-threatening.  
   * **Erythrodermic Psoriasis**: Generalized erythema and scaling over \>80% of the body surface, associated with systemic complications.  
3. **Nail Involvement**:  
   * Nail pitting, subungual hyperkeratosis, and onycholysis.  
   * Common in patients with psoriatic arthritis.  
4. **Psoriatic Arthritis**:  
   * Occurs in up to 30% of psoriasis patients.  
   * Patterns include oligoarticular, symmetric polyarthritis, DIP arthropathy, arthritis mutilans, and spondylitis.

### **Integrative Therapy**

#### **1\. Skin Care**

* Use **gentle cleansers** and thick emollients to prevent trauma and Koebnerization.  
* **Oatmeal baths** or colloidal oatmeal for itch relief.  
* Natural oils (coconut, avocado, almond) improve hydration and reduce inflammation.

#### **2\. Ultraviolet Therapy**

* **Narrow-band UVB**: Effective for plaque psoriasis with lower cancer risk than PUVA.  
* **Dead Sea Therapy**: Combines climatotherapy, sun exposure, and mineral baths.

#### **3\. Nutrition**

* **Mediterranean Diet**: High in extra virgin olive oil, fish, vegetables, and nuts.  
* **Weight Loss**: Reduces systemic inflammation and improves treatment outcomes.  
* **Gluten-Free Diet**: Consider for patients with elevated celiac markers or GI symptoms.

#### **4\. Supplements**

* **Fish Oil**: Omega-3 fatty acids (EPA/DHA) reduce inflammation (2–3 g/day).  
* **Curcumin**: Anti-inflammatory effects; dose of 1500 mg/day.  
* **Vitamin D**: Supplement deficiency (1000–2000 IU/day).  
* **Zinc**: Limited evidence; 15–30 mg/day.

---

### **Conventional Therapy**

#### **Topical Treatments**

1. **Corticosteroids**:  
   * First-line therapy for mild-to-moderate psoriasis.  
   * Use lower-potency steroids for sensitive areas (face, groin).  
2. **Vitamin D Analogs (Calcipotriene)**:  
   * Inhibits keratinocyte proliferation (apply twice daily; max 100 g/week).  
3. **Tazarotene**:  
   * Topical retinoid that normalizes keratinocyte differentiation.  
4. **Tar-Based Products**:  
   * Anti-inflammatory and antipruritic.  
5. **Keratolytics**:  
   * Salicylic acid or urea for plaque reduction.

#### **Phototherapy**

* **UVB Therapy**: 2–3 times/week.  
* **PUVA**: Effective but higher risk of long-term skin cancer.

#### **Systemic Treatments**

1. **Methotrexate**:  
   * Weekly dosing (10–15 mg/week).  
   * Monitor for hepatotoxicity and bone marrow suppression.  
2. **Cyclosporine**:  
   * Rapid onset for severe cases (5 mg/kg/day initially).  
   * Monitor for hypertension and renal dysfunction.  
3. **Biologics**:  
   * TNF-alpha inhibitors: Adalimumab, etanercept, infliximab.  
   * IL-17/IL-23 inhibitors: Secukinumab, ustekinumab.  
4. **Apremilast (PDE4 Inhibitor)**:  
   * Oral option with mild side effects; no lab monitoring required.

### **Mind–Body Therapy**

* **Meditation**: Reduces stress and improves treatment response.  
* **Hypnosis**: Effective for highly hypnotizable patients.

---

### **Prevention Prescription**

* Maintain a balanced lifestyle, minimize stress, and avoid triggers (e.g., smoking, alcohol).  
* Use emollients regularly to hydrate skin and maintain barrier function.  
* Consider early treatment of infections to prevent flares.  
* Avoid rapid tapering of systemic corticosteroids and medications that exacerbate psoriasis.

### **Summary of Alcohol Use Disorder (AUD) and Substance Abuse Management**

#### **Definition and Scope**

* **AUD**: A chronic medical condition characterized by problematic drinking patterns meeting DSM-5 criteria. Severity is determined by the number of criteria met within 12 months (mild: 2-3, moderate: 4-5, severe: ≥6).  
* Substance abuse includes alcohol, tobacco, opiates, cocaine, marijuana, and others, often exacerbating chronic diseases and complicating primary care management.

---

#### **Key Components of Diagnosis and Risk Assessment**

1. **Screening Tools**:  
   * **AUDIT-C**: A validated, brief screening for hazardous alcohol use and AUD.  
     * Scores ≥4 in men and \>2 in women indicate risky drinking.  
   * **DSM-5 Criteria**: A minimum of two criteria met within a year confirms AUD.  
2. **Risk Levels** (Box 83.2):  
   * Abstinence: No alcohol use.  
   * Moderate use:  
     * Men: ≤2 drinks/day.  
     * Women/Older adults: ≤1 drink/day.  
   * Risky use:  
     * Men: \>4 drinks/day or \>14 drinks/week.  
     * Women/Older adults: \>3 drinks/day or \>7 drinks/week.

---

#### **Pathophysiology of Addiction**

* Addiction involves alterations in brain reward pathways, primarily the dopaminergic system, leading to euphoria, cravings, and dependence.  
* Influenced by biological, environmental, social, cultural, and genetic factors.

---

#### **Treatment Approaches**

1. **Pharmaceutical Interventions**:  
   * **Alcohol**:  
     * **Withdrawal**: Benzodiazepines (e.g., diazepam, clonazepam) for anxiety and sleep disturbances; phenobarbital for seizures.  
     * **Craving/Relapse Prevention**:  
       * **Naltrexone**: Reduces euphoria from alcohol.  
       * **Acamprosate**: Reduces anxiety and cravings.  
       * **Disulfiram**: Produces unpleasant effects with alcohol consumption.  
       * **Topiramate**: Reduces heavy drinking and promotes abstinence.  
   * **Tobacco**:  
     * Nicotine replacement therapy (NRT), **bupropion**, and **varenicline**.  
   * **Opiates**:  
     * Methadone and **buprenorphine** for maintenance.  
     * **Naloxone** for overdose management.  
   * **Cocaine**:  
     * Antidepressants and dopamine agonists (e.g., amantadine).  
   * **Marijuana**:  
     * Limited pharmaceutical options.  
2. **Complementary and Alternative Therapies (CAM)**:  
   * **Botanicals**:  
     * **Kudzu**: Reduces alcohol consumption in heavy drinkers.  
     * **St. John’s Wort**: May aid in managing coexisting depression.  
     * **Valerian/Kava kava**: Help with anxiety (monitor liver toxicity for kava).  
   * **Acupuncture**:  
     * Effective in reducing cravings and promoting relaxation (e.g., NADA protocol).  
   * **Mind-Body Therapies**:  
     * Meditation, biofeedback, hypnosis, guided imagery, and yoga to reduce stress and improve coping mechanisms.  
   * **Spirituality**:  
     * Encouraging connection to a higher power or spiritual practices, integral to programs like AA.  
   * **Twelve-Step Programs**:  
     * AA, Narcotics Anonymous, and Al-Anon provide structured support.  
3. **Culturally Specific Interventions**:  
   * For Native American populations, traditional practices like sweat lodges and talking circles address spiritual and emotional balance.

---

#### **Management Principles**

* **For Withdrawal**:  
  * Use benzodiazepines or methadone under close supervision.  
  * Manage cravings with targeted pharmacological agents.  
* **For Relapse Prevention**:  
  * Combine pharmaceuticals with psychosocial support.  
* **Integrated Care**:  
  * Address coexisting anxiety, depression, and nutritional deficiencies.  
  * Provide holistic support, including CAM therapies and lifestyle interventions.  
* **Prevention**:  
  * Regular screening, early intervention, and counseling on risks associated with substance use.

---

#### **Practical Steps for Providers**

1. Use validated tools like AUDIT-C for screening.  
2. Match pharmacological treatments to individual needs (e.g., cravings, withdrawal, or negative feedback).  
3. Refer to specialized programs for detoxification and long-term recovery.  
4. Incorporate CAM and culturally relevant practices into treatment plans.  
5. Encourage participation in structured support groups like AA.

---

#### **Conclusion**

A comprehensive, integrative approach that includes pharmaceuticals, CAM therapies, and psychosocial support is essential for effectively managing AUD and substance abuse. Early intervention, tailored treatments, and cultural sensitivity can improve outcomes and foster long-term recovery.

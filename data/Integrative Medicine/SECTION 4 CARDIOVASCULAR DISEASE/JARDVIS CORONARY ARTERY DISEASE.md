### **Key Takeaways on Cardiovascular Disease (CVD) Management**

#### **1\. Pathophysiology Insights**

* Acute coronary events often arise from "mild" lesions (\<50% stenosis), emphasizing prevention of lesion formation rather than solely addressing severe blockages.  
* Triggers include genetic (e.g., dyslipidemia, diabetes) and lifestyle/environmental factors (e.g., nutrition, inactivity, stress, smoking).

---

#### **2\. Integrative Therapy Approaches**

##### **Nutrition**

* **Mediterranean Diet**:  
  * **Proven Benefits**: Reduces cardiovascular events by 30%–70% in both primary and secondary prevention.  
  * **Components**:  
    * **Vegetables**: 5 servings/day (e.g., leafy greens reduce CHD risk by 23% per serving).  
    * **Fruits**: 2 servings/day.  
    * **Whole Grains**: Emphasize intact grains; avoid refined carbohydrates.  
    * **Fish**: 2 servings/week.  
    * **Nuts**: 4 servings/week; reduces CHD risk by 37%.  
    * Limit red meat and refined grains.  
* **Supplements for Lipid Management**:  
  * Psyllium (10 g/day): Reduces LDL-C by 7%.  
  * Stanols/sterols (2 g/day): Lowers LDL-C by 12%.  
  * Red yeast rice (1200–2400 mg/day): Reduces LDL-C by 20%–30%.

##### **Exercise**

* **Aerobic Activity**:  
  * Moderate-intensity walking (30 min/day) reduces CVD risk by 18%.  
  * Aim for 150 minutes/week of moderate or vigorous exercise.  
* **Resistance Training**:  
  * At least 30 min/week improves strength and reduces risk by 23%.

##### **Mind-Body Interventions**

* **Stress Reduction**: Address anxiety and depression with:  
  * Meditation, yoga, breathing exercises, and biofeedback.  
  * These reduce stress hormones and improve cardiac function.  
* **Emotional Well-Being**:  
  * Optimism reduces cardiovascular mortality by 55%.  
  * Encourage spiritual or social connections.

##### **Anti-Inflammatory Lifestyle**

* Smoking cessation: Strongly recommended.  
* Weight management: Focus on balanced, nutrient-dense diets.

---

#### **3\. Pharmaceutical and Supplementary Interventions**

##### **Pharmaceuticals**

* Proven treatments for symptomatic coronary artery disease include:  
  * Aspirin, beta-blockers, nitrates, calcium channel blockers.  
  * Statins: Essential for LDL-C reduction and plaque stabilization.  
  * ACE inhibitors: Provide cardioprotective effects beyond hypertension management.  
* Address **statin intolerance** with:  
  * Alternative statins (e.g., rosuvastatin, pravastatin).  
  * CoQ10 (100–200 mg/day) and vitamin D (\>30 ng/mL).

##### **Supplements**

* **Fish Oil**:  
  * Prevention dose: 1000 mg EPA/DHA/day.  
  * Treatment dose for hypertriglyceridemia: 1000–4000 mg EPA/DHA/day.  
* **Vitamin D**:  
  * Replace deficiencies to improve cardiovascular outcomes.

---

#### **4\. Procedures and Advanced Therapies**

* **Angioplasty/Stenting**:  
  * Reserved for acute myocardial infarction or unstable angina.  
  * For stable CVD, medical therapy is equally effective for survival.  
* **Enhanced External Counterpulsation (EECP)**:  
  * Noninvasive option for refractory angina; improves symptoms in 70%+ of patients.  
* **Chelation Therapy**:  
  * Promising for select patients, particularly diabetics, pending further research.

---

### **Actionable Steps for Patients**

1. **Adopt a Mediterranean Diet**: Prioritize vegetables, fruits, fish, and whole grains while limiting red meat.  
2. **Engage in Regular Physical Activity**:  
   * 30 min/day of moderate aerobic exercise.  
   * Incorporate resistance training weekly.  
3. **Quit Smoking**: Use counseling or pharmacological aids if necessary.  
4. **Manage Stress**: Explore mind-body therapies like meditation or yoga.  
5. **Follow Lipid Management Guidelines**:  
   * Use statins as prescribed; address intolerance with alternatives.  
   * Supplement with fiber, stanols, or red yeast rice if appropriate.  
6. **Monitor and Optimize Vitamin D Levels**: Aim for \>30 ng/mL.  
7. **Explore Advanced Therapies**:  
   * EECP for refractory angina.  
   * Discuss chelation therapy for post-MI or high-risk patients.

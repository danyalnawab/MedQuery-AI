### **Pathophysiology**

* **Asthma Overview**: A chronic respiratory disorder characterized by airway obstruction, bronchial hyperresponsiveness, and inflammation. Over 25 million people in the U.S. are affected, with 18 million being adults.  
* **Mechanisms**:  
  * Chronic airway inflammation involves **mast cells, eosinophils**, and **Th2 CD4+ T-lymphocytes**, leading to epithelial damage and airway remodeling.  
  * Allergic antibody **IgE** plays a significant role in the clinical manifestation of asthma and allergies.

---

### **Risk Factors and Triggers**

* **Genetics and Environment**: A combination of genetic predisposition and environmental exposures (e.g., allergens, viral infections, pollutants).  
* **Triggers** (Table 29.1):  
  * **Allergens**: Dust mites, pets, pollens.  
  * **Environmental**: Smoke, air pollutants, occupational exposures.  
  * **Lifestyle**: Exercise, emotional anxiety.  
  * **Health Conditions**: Gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD), viral infections.

---

### **Management and Integrative Therapy**

#### **Lifestyle and Environmental Control**

* **Trigger Reduction**: Use HEPA filters, clean frequently, and avoid secondhand smoke and pets in sensitive individuals.  
* **Dust Mite Control**: Enclose bedding, remove carpeting, wash sheets weekly in hot water.  
* **Nutrition**:  
  * **Elimination Diet**: Identify and remove potential allergens, including sulfites, dairy, and food additives.  
  * **Mediterranean Diet**: Rich in fruits, vegetables, olive oil, and fish, shown to help prevent and manage asthma.  
  * **Supplements**: Include **omega-3 fatty acids**, **vitamin D**, and **antioxidants** like vitamin C and E.

#### **Exercise**

* Regular physical activity improves asthma control and overall health. Swimming and non-chlorinated environments are preferable for some patients.

---

### **Mind-Body Therapies**

* **Breathing Techniques**:  
  * **Buteyko Method**: Focuses on reduced respiratory rate and improved biomechanics.  
  * **Yoga**: Combines physical exercise with controlled breathing and relaxation.  
* **Cognitive Therapies**:  
  * **Guided Imagery and Hypnosis**: Useful for relaxation and symptom control, particularly in motivated patients.  
  * **Journaling**: Documenting stress and asthma symptoms has been shown to reduce exacerbations.

---

### **Botanicals and Supplements**

* **Coleus forskohlii**: Acts as a bronchodilator; dosage: 50 mg two to three times/day.  
* **Boswellia serrata**: Anti-inflammatory; dosage: 300 mg three times/day.  
* **Pycnogenol**: Improves lung function and reduces symptoms; dosage: 30–100 mg/day.  
* **Magnesium**: Reduces bronchospasm; dosage: 200–400 mg/day.  
* **Fish Oil**: Omega-3 fatty acids to reduce inflammation; dosage: 500 mg two to three times/day.

---

### **Pharmaceuticals**

* **Bronchodilators**:  
  * **Short-acting beta-agonists (SABAs)**: Albuterol, Levalbuterol.  
  * **Long-acting beta-agonists (LABAs)**: Salmeterol, Formoterol.  
* **Anti-inflammatory Medications**:  
  * Inhaled corticosteroids (ICS): Fluticasone, Budesonide.  
  * Leukotriene modifiers: Montelukast (10 mg daily).

---

### **Biomechanical Approaches**

* **Massage**: Shown to improve lung function in children with asthma.  
* **Osteopathy and Chiropractic**: Focus on structural and functional alignment to enhance respiratory health.

---

### **Prevention and Therapeutic Review**

1. **Eliminate Triggers**: Environmental allergens, stress, and dietary sensitivities.  
2. **Nutrition**: Promote antioxidant-rich diets and omega-3 fatty acids.  
3. **Pharmacological Therapy**: Inhaled corticosteroids and bronchodilators as first-line treatments.  
4. **Mind-Body Techniques**: Breathing exercises, guided imagery, and journaling for stress and symptom management.  
5. **Complementary Therapies**: Include botanicals, acupuncture, and homeopathy under professional supervision.
